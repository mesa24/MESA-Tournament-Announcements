# MESA Esports Tournament Rulebook 2026

> **Version:** 2.0 — 2026  
> **Operated by:** MESA Tournament Administration  
> **Contact:** mkhantamir@mesa.mn

---

_This rulebook is effective as of April 1, 2026 and supersedes all previous MESA tournament regulations. All participants are bound by these rules from the moment they register for any MESA event on or after this date._

## Foreword

This document sets out the rules and regulations that govern all MESA esports competitions. Every participant — player, coach, substitute, or team representative — is bound by these rules from the moment they register for any MESA event, regardless of the game title.

Sections 1 through 7 and Sections 9 through 13 are **universal** and apply to all MESA competitions. **Section 8** contains game-specific technical rules and currently covers **CS2**. Additional game-specific sections will be appended as MESA expands to other titles.

The tournament administration retains final authority in all matters. In exceptional cases, decisions may be made that are not explicitly described in this rulebook in order to uphold the principles of fair play and sportsmanship. MESA reserves the right to amend, supplement, or override any rule where necessary to protect the integrity of competition.

## 1. Definitions

**Participant** — Any person (player, coach, substitute, or support staff) registered to a team for a MESA event.

**Team** — The legal entity or designated representative holding a MESA event licence on behalf of a roster.

**Roster** — The five starting players registered for an event, plus any registered substitutes and coach.

**Core Players** — The players whose presence defines a qualifying or invited roster. A team must retain a majority (at minimum three out of five) of its core players throughout the entirety of the event and all of its qualifying stages.

**Open Qualifier** — A publicly accessible qualifying stage open to any eligible team.

**Closed Qualifier** — An invitation-only qualifying stage restricted to teams meeting defined criteria.

**Main Event** — The principal competition stage following the conclusion of all qualifying stages.

**Event Licence** — The official permission granted by MESA to a participant to compete in a specific event. Licences are event-specific and non-transferable.

**Penalty Points** — A cumulative disciplinary measure resulting in prize money deductions and potential further sanctions.

**Tournament Administration** — MESA staff responsible for overseeing operations, ruling on disputes, and enforcing this rulebook.

**VRS** — Valve Regional Standings, the official Valve-published ranking used for seeding and invitations.

## 2. Participation Conditions

### 2.1 Age Requirement

All participants must be at least **sixteen (16) years of age** at the start of the first mandatory day of any MESA competition, including media days and qualifying stages.

### 2.2 Regional Eligibility

A team's home region is determined by the **citizenship of the majority of its players**. If no majority exists due to a tie, the team is assigned to the region with greater representation in the most recent Valve Major. In the Americas, the priority is South America over North America; in Asia, East Asia takes priority over Oceania, then West Asia.

A team may not attempt to qualify for the same event through more than one region. Individual players may play in a different region only under an explicit exception granted in writing by MESA.

### 2.3 Platform Account Requirements

All participants must have a registered and verified account on **mesa.mn**, MESA's official tournament platform, to compete in any MESA event. Player registration, roster submission, match scheduling, and result reporting are all conducted through mesa.mn. Players must submit their Steam ID64 and mesa.mn profile during registration. A player may only compete using the game account they have registered on mesa.mn. Any change of game account must be pre-approved by the tournament administration before the relevant match.

### 2.4 Player Details

Upon request, participants must provide the following information: full legal name, contact details, date of birth, game account details, Steam ID64, and a photo. Failure to provide accurate information may result in disqualification.

### 2.5 Nicknames

Players must use their own registered nickname without any additions. Nicknames registered with Valve for sponsored events must be used consistently across all MESA matches. Nickname changes must be requested by email to the tournament administration. Nicknames containing political, religious, offensive, or otherwise inappropriate content will be rejected.

### 2.6 Sponsor Restrictions

Sponsors associated solely or prominently with pornography, illegal drug use, or other adult content that could harm MESA's reputation are not permitted in connection with any MESA event.

### 2.7 Confidentiality

All correspondence with tournament administration — including email, match channels, and any other communication — is strictly confidential. Publishing such material without written consent from MESA is prohibited.

### 2.8 Additional Agreements

MESA does not enforce private agreements between participants. Any agreement that contradicts this rulebook is void with respect to MESA proceedings.

## 3. Team Licences and Organisation Rules

### 3.1 Event Licences

MESA grants an event licence to participants who have qualified through an open or closed qualifier, been directly invited, or earned participation through another MESA-recognised process. Licences are valid for the specific event only and may not be transferred to another entity without explicit approval.

### 3.2 One Licence Per Organisation Per Event

A legal entity may hold only **one licence per competition**. No organisation or its affiliates may hold more than one licence in the same competition bracket. It is strictly forbidden for an organisation to change which roster represents them once the event has begun.

### 3.3 Multiple Rosters from the Same Organisation

Organisations operating multiple rosters must clearly distinguish them with different names (e.g., "MESA Blue" and "MESA Red", or "MESA" and "MESA Academy"). The following restrictions apply:

- A team may not participate in a MESA event if another roster from the same organisation has already been invited to or qualified for the main event.
- A team may not participate in a closed qualifier if another roster from the same organisation has already been invited to or qualified for that same closed qualifier.
- Two rosters from the same organisation may enter different open qualifiers, but once one roster secures qualification, the other is immediately removed from the tournament.
- If a second roster from the same organisation qualifies for a stage where the first roster is already participating, the second roster forfeits its slot to the next eligible team.

### 3.4 Conflict of Interest

Players and coaches may not hold any financial interest in the success of any other team competing in the same MESA competition. This includes shared management, shared ownership, or any business entanglement. Participants must declare any potential conflict of interest to MESA before competing. Tournament staff and administrators may not hold any business relationship with any participating team without prior written disclosure to and approval from MESA.

### 3.5 Team Name and Branding

Team names may consist only of the organisation name and, where permitted, a single title sponsor. Names may not include extensions such as "CS team." All team names, logos, and branding must comply with professional standards and may not contain offensive, discriminatory, or hateful content, political or religious messaging, sexually explicit material, content that infringes intellectual property rights, or misleading information. MESA may require a team to change its name or branding at any time.

Title sponsor name changes must be applied to the entire organisation and all its teams. A request for a title sponsor name change must be submitted before the roster deadline of the first tournament in which the new name is to appear. Such a change may only be requested once in a twelve (12) month period.

### 3.6 Licence Transfer

A licence holder may request a transfer to another entity for adequate reason. Approval rests entirely at MESA's discretion. Any participant who voluntarily surrenders their event licence may be excluded from future MESA licences in the same competition.

## 4. Roster Management

### 4.1 Roster Submission

Teams must submit their roster through **mesa.mn** before their first qualifier match. A valid submitted roster must include:

- Five (5) starting players
- Up to two (2) substitute players (optional but recommended)
- One (1) coach (optional; the coach may serve as a substitute if fewer than two substitutes are registered)

All persons listed on the roster are considered part of the official team, even if they have not competed in any match. Each participant must submit their full name, date of birth, Steam ID64, and game account via mesa.mn. Rosters are made public automatically by MESA once the first qualifier match begins. If a team fails to complete their roster submission on mesa.mn, MESA reserves the right to publish whatever information is available.

### 4.2 Roster Submission Deadline

The roster must be fully submitted on mesa.mn **before the start of a team's first match in the open qualifier**. The roster active at the moment of a team's first match is treated as their locked roster for the remainder of the event, subject to Sections 4.3 through 4.6. If a team has not completed their roster submission by the time their first match is scheduled to begin, they may be declared a no-show and forfeit that match.

### 4.3 Core Player Retention — The Majority Rule

Once a team plays its first match in any qualifying stage of an event, the lineup used in that first match is fixed as the **reference lineup**. From that point until the conclusion of the main event:

- The team's active lineup must contain a **majority (at least three out of five) of the original reference lineup** at all times.
- This rule applies continuously across all qualifier stages and through to the end of the main event.
- An organisation that failed to qualify through one roster may **not** enter a new or replacement roster to continue participating in the same event.
- Invited teams are treated as having played throughout the entire qualifying process up to the stage at which they were invited. The invited lineup at the time of invitation (or the specific lineup named in the invitation letter) serves as their reference lineup.

### 4.4 Player Transfer Restrictions Within an Event

The following restrictions apply to all player movements across the entirety of a single event and its qualifying stages:

**i)** A player who competed in and **qualified from an open qualifier** for an event:

- May not participate in another open qualifier for the same event with any other team.
- May not play in any closed qualifier for the same event with another team.

**ii)** A player who competed in and **qualified from a closed qualifier** for an event:

- May not participate in any further qualifier for the same event.
- May not play in the main event with a different team.

These restrictions are absolute. Violation results in immediate disqualification of the offending team and suspension of the involved player.

### 4.5 Adding New Players

Any addition to the roster during an event must comply with the majority rule (Rule 4.3) and the transfer restrictions (Rule 4.4). Before a new player may be officially added, the tournament administration must be informed by email with the following details: nickname, full legal name, date of birth, and game account. This notification must be provided within a reasonable timeframe before the next match. Once approved, the team must resubmit its roster form and update all relevant platform profiles.

### 4.6 Emergency Additions

If a team is unable to field a full lineup due to a genuine and unforeseen emergency, an emergency addition may be requested. All requests must include supporting documentation and will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis at the tournament administration's sole discretion. Emergency additions remain subject to Rule 4.3 and Rule 4.4. All costs related to getting the new player to the event are the team's responsibility.

### 4.7 Substitutions

Only players listed on the submitted roster may be substituted in. The opposing team and tournament administration must be notified before any substitution takes place. Any substitution that would violate eligibility rules constitutes a forfeit of the match.

## 5. Match Administration

### 5.1 Match Schedule

All matches must begin as stated in the tournament schedule. Any rescheduling requires approval from the tournament administration and, where applicable, the opposing team. Participants must be present and ready to play at least **ten (10) minutes before** the scheduled match start time.

### 5.2 Punctuality — Online Matches

- **−10 minutes:** All participants must be on the server and ready.
- **−10 to −5 minutes late:** One (1) penalty point.
- **−5 minutes to start time:** A further one (1) penalty point.
- **At start time, if still not ready:** One (1) additional penalty point, plus one (1) penalty point every two (2) minutes thereafter.
- **+15 minutes from start time:** The participant is declared a no-show and forfeits the match. An additional five (5) penalty points are applied.

### 5.3 Punctuality — Offline Matches

Players must arrive at their designated tournament area **no later than fifty (50) minutes** before their scheduled match start time. Coaches must arrive **no later than thirty (30) minutes** before match start time. Participants who know they will be late must notify the tournament administration as early as possible.

- **First 10 minutes late:** Warning.
- **Every 5 minutes thereafter:** One (1) penalty point, up to eight (8) penalty points.
- **Beyond the scheduled start time:** 1.5 penalty points per two (2) minutes, up to twelve (12) penalty points.
- **+5 minutes past start time:** The on-time team is awarded the **right to choose starting side** on any map where side selection would normally be determined by a knife round.
- **+10 minutes past start time:** The on-time team is additionally awarded the **right to choose starting side on the decider map**, if the series reaches one.
- **+15 minutes from start time:** Match forfeit, plus five (5) additional penalty points.

These side-selection advantages are applied automatically regardless of seeding or veto outcome. They stack — a team that arrives more than ten (10) minutes late forfeits both rights.

If a team attends an offline event but forfeits all of its matches, it is treated as having withdrawn from the event.

### 5.4 No-Show

For a single-map match, the match is forfeited in full after fifteen (15) minutes. For multi-map series, one map is awarded to the opponent after the initial fifteen (15) minutes; a further map is awarded every fifteen (15) minutes thereafter until the series is won by the opponent.

### 5.5 Map Veto

The higher seed has the choice of whether to initiate or defer the veto. Veto procedures for each format are as follows:

**Best-of-One (BO1):**

1. Team A bans — Team B bans — Team A bans — Team B bans — Team A bans — Team B bans — Remaining map is played.

**Best-of-Three (BO3):**

1. Team A bans — Team B bans — Team A picks — Team B picks — Team A bans — Team B bans — Remaining map is the decider.

**Best-of-Five (BO5):**

1. Team A bans — Team B bans — Team A picks — Team B picks — Team A picks — Team B picks — Remaining map is the fifth map if needed.

Starting side on any map is determined by the winner of the knife round, unless otherwise stated.

### 5.6 Seeding

Tournament seeding is based on the **Valve Regional Standings (VRS)** version announced for the event, or the latest available VRS publication before the first qualifier begins if no specific version is announced. Seeding is determined once and applied for the duration of the relevant stage. No adjustment is made for roster changes occurring after the seeding date.

### 5.7 Match Protests

A protest is a formal communication to report problems that affected or are affecting the outcome of a match. Protests must include detailed information about the nature of the issue, how it arose, and when it occurred. Only one representative per team may be involved in the protest process. The deadline for filing a protest is the **earliest** of the following: twelve (12) hours after the scheduled match start time, the beginning of either team's next match (with a minimum ten-minute separation), or the end of the event day during offline events.

### 5.8 Result Challenges

If a team wishes to challenge the outcome of a match, they must formally submit their challenge to the tournament administration **within fifteen (15) minutes** of the match concluding. The challenge must include a description of the issue and any available evidence. Submitting within this window is sufficient to preserve the team's right to a ruling — the challenge is considered filed regardless of whether an administrator has responded before the window expires. The tournament administration may review the case and issue a ruling at any point after the challenge is filed, and may reverse the result or take other appropriate action if warranted. Challenges not submitted within fifteen (15) minutes are not eligible for review.

## 6. Event Rules — Offline Competitions

### 6.1 Equipment

MESA provides monitors, computers, SSDs, and noise-cancelling headphones. Participants must bring their own: keyboard, mouse, mousepad, in-ear headphones with sufficient cable length, and any required sound cards. All equipment is subject to approval by the tournament administration. MESA may prohibit any device deemed to provide an unfair competitive advantage. Participants wishing to use more than one unit of the same equipment must obtain prior approval. Modifying BIOS settings on MESA-provided computers is strictly prohibited.

**SOCD (Simultaneous Opposing Cardinal Directions) features** — including Snap Tap, Rappy Snappy, or any equivalent mechanism — are strictly prohibited. Violations may result in penalties ranging from a default map loss to tournament disqualification.

### 6.2 Configurations and Drivers

All participants must submit their configurations and required driver details (software name and version) by the deadline specified by the tournament administration. Participants who fail to submit configs may be required to set up manually on-site without additional time. Submitted config folders may only include official CS2 convar configs, key bind configs, video settings, and one additional configuration file. Launch options, NVIDIA settings, and a Spotify playlist may also be included in a supplementary text file.

### 6.3 Technical Checklist

After completing the setup process, every player must sign the technical checklist provided by the tournament administration. By signing, the participant confirms they are ready to play as scheduled. Issues that would have been identified through the checklist may result in a penalty point and loss of any additional setup time.

### 6.4 Clothing and Appearance

All players must wear matching team attire with long trousers and closed shoes. Shorts, flip-flops, and open footwear are not permitted. Headwear is forbidden. Exceptions for medical or religious reasons must be requested by email at least forty-eight (48) hours before competition begins.

Minor violations result in a minimum fine of **$250**. Major violations (offensive content, another team's branding) will prevent the match from starting until the issue is resolved. If MESA provides replacement clothing, the cost is deducted from prize money. Any delay caused by a clothing violation is penalised under the punctuality rules.

### 6.5 Tournament Areas and Conduct

The following apply within all tournament areas:

- Only food and beverages approved by MESA may be consumed in the playing area. No branded drinks may be displayed on stage.
- Electronic devices other than gaming peripherals (including mobile phones, smart watches, tablets, and laptops) are forbidden in the playing area unless approved by the tournament administration. Participants may use a mobile phone to log in to Steam, but must immediately hand it to an official.
- Players must not communicate with anyone outside the match during live play. Communication in the playing area is limited to the five active players and the registered coach.
- Participants must not leave the server during a match without explicit permission from a tournament official.

### 6.6 Setup and Warm-Up

Teams are provided with a minimum of **thirty (30) minutes** for setup and warm-up before their first match of each day. No additional setup time is provided for subsequent matches on the same day. Setup time is not reduced due to mandatory stage activities such as walkouts.

### 6.7 Media Obligations

MESA may require participants to take part in interviews, press conferences, photography sessions, or video sessions. Participation is mandatory and may not be refused. Most events include a mandatory media day. MESA has the right to photograph, record, broadcast, and distribute all match content and participant likenesses without restriction.

Participants failing to fulfil media obligations may receive penalty points or fines.

### 6.8 Winners Ceremony

All teams finishing the event are expected to attend the closing ceremony as directed by the tournament administration. Failure to attend without valid reason may result in penalty points.

### 6.9 Arrival Requirements

Participants must arrive at the designated player hotel or venue by the date communicated as the Arrival Date. Late arrivals are penalised under the punctuality rules. Exceptions may be granted for documented medical or flight emergencies; all requests must be submitted by email before the Arrival Date.

### 6.10 Damage to Property

Participants who damage or soil rooms, furniture, equipment, or any other items provided by MESA or the venue will incur a minimum fine of **$500**. This fine may be increased based on restoration costs and any reputational harm caused.

## 7. Event Rules — Online Competitions

### 7.1 Server Selection

Server location for online matches is determined by mutual agreement between the two teams following the veto. If no agreement is reached, the tournament administration selects the server based on both teams' average ping, measured directly from the server. This decision is final.

### 7.2 Camera Obligations

When required by the tournament administration, all players and coaches must have a functioning camera feed active throughout the duration of each map, including pre-match. Cameras must be active at least **thirty (30) minutes** before match start time. Cameras may be turned off between maps but must be turned back on at least **five (5) minutes** before the next map begins.

Fines for missing camera feeds per map are as follows:

| Proportion of Lineup Without Camera | Fine                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 20% (1 of 5 players)                | $50 + 0.25% of prize winnings  |
| 40% (2 of 5 players)                | $100 + 0.50% of prize winnings |
| 60% (3 of 5 players)                | $150 + 0.75% of prize winnings |
| 80% (4 of 5 players)                | $200 + 1.00% of prize winnings |
| 100% (all 5 players)                | $250 + 1.25% of prize winnings |
| Coach missing                       | Additional $50 + 0.25%         |

Repeat offenders (3 or more maps in the same competition) face increased fines: +100% for the third offence, +200% for the fourth, and +300% for the fifth and beyond.

### 7.3 Voice Communication

The official voice communication platform for MESA online events is Discord. Match communication is restricted to the five active players and the registered coach. Additional personnel may be present in the voice channel as passive listeners only (muted at all times). MESA reserves the right to monitor and record all communications during matches.

### 7.4 Technical Pauses

Teams are limited to a total of **ten (10) minutes** of technical pauses per map. After the allowance is exhausted, a team may use a tactical timeout to extend resolution time. If no timeouts remain, the team must substitute or play through. Technical pauses may be called at any time but take effect at the start of the next freeze time. Communication between players and coaches is forbidden during technical pauses.

### 7.5 Match Interruptions

If a player suffers a serious technical issue (e.g. internet failure, power outage) that cannot be resolved quickly, the team may request a substitution from the submitted roster, at the discretion of the Tournament Operations Manager. Any resulting pause must not exceed **five (5) minutes**.

## 8. Technical Rules and Game Settings

> _This section applies exclusively to CS2 competitions. All other sections of this rulebook apply universally across all MESA events regardless of title._

### 8.1 Game Version

MESA uses a **locked version** of CS2 for each event, selected via the Steam beta branch system. The specific version to be used will be communicated to all teams **no later than one (1) week before the first match** of the event. Once locked, the game version will not change for the remainder of the event unless it contains a critical bug or severe balance issue, as determined solely by the tournament administration.

If Valve releases a mandatory update that overrides the locked version during an event, MESA will assess the impact and communicate a decision to all teams as soon as possible.

### 8.2 Map Pool

MESA uses the current **Valve-approved active duty map pool** unless otherwise specified in the event announcement. If Valve updates the map pool during a season, MESA will announce whether the change applies to the current event or takes effect from the next event. The map pool in use will be confirmed in the event-specific announcement.

### 8.3 GOTV and Match Demos

GOTV broadcast is at the discretion of the tournament administration and will be enabled for selected matches only. When GOTV is active, the spectator delay is set to **two (2) minutes** to prevent ghosting. Teams may not use any GOTV feed or spectator stream to gain in-match information under any circumstances.

MESA servers automatically record all match demos. Players may request access to their match demo after the conclusion of the match by contacting the tournament administration. MESA will submit completed match demos to HLTV as soon as possible following each event.

### 8.4 Knife Round and Side Selection

All maps begin with a **knife round** to determine side selection, subject to the following rules:

- The winning team's captain must select their starting side using the `!stay` or `!switch` commands within **one (1) minute** of the knife round concluding. If no selection is made within this window, the starting sides are assigned randomly by the server.
- In BO3 and BO5 matches, a knife round is played at the start of every map including the decider.
- **When a team picks a map during the veto, the opposing team chooses the starting side on that map.** No knife round is played on a picked map; the non-picking team's side selection replaces it.
- **Punctuality advantages override knife round outcomes.** If a team has been awarded side selection rights under Rule 5.3 due to the opponent's late arrival, those rights apply to the relevant map regardless of the knife round result.

### 8.5 Tactical Timeouts

Each team receives **three (3) tactical timeouts** of thirty (30) seconds per regulation map. Timeouts may be used in separate rounds or consecutively. To call a timeout, the team must use the in-game `callvote` function. Coaches may communicate with players freely during tactical timeouts.

After the regulation period concludes and overtime begins, tactical timeouts are **reset to one (1) per team**. After every six (6) overtime rounds, if no winner has been determined, timeouts reset again to one (1) per team. This cycle repeats until a winner is decided.

### 8.6 Restricted Weapons

MESA enforces the current Valve-mandated restricted weapons list. If Valve adds a weapon to the restricted list within an unreasonable timeframe before an event, MESA may extend the restriction to cover that weapon and will inform all teams in advance.

### 8.7 Permitted and Forbidden In-Game Settings

**Permitted:**

- Jump throw binds and aliases
- `r_cleardecals` as a bind
- Grenade drop binds
- Buy binds of any kind
- Toggle and jump scripts
- In-game brightness, gamma, contrast, and digital vibrance settings
- Mouse acceleration, pointer precision, and sensitivity adjustments
- Steam overlay (FPS counter and main overlay only, provided no competitive advantage is gained)

**Forbidden:**

- Any script that automates movement, aiming, or purchasing beyond a single key input
- SOCD (Simultaneous Opposing Cardinal Directions) features of any kind — including Snap Tap, Rappy Snappy, Wooting SOCD, or any mechanism producing equivalent outputs
- Overlays that display information on top of the game client, including but not limited to RivaTuner, Overwolf, and Nvidia SLI Display
- Any custom game file that modifies HUDs, crosshairs, scoreboards, player models, weapon models, or sprites
- Third-party software or hardware that provides aim assistance, wallhacks, radar hacks, or any other form of information not available to all players

If a player is unsure whether a specific script, bind, or setting is permitted, they must contact the tournament administration **before** using it. Unsanctioned use will be treated as a violation.

### 8.8 Configurations and Peripheral Drivers

For **offline events**, all participants must submit their configuration files and a list of required peripheral drivers (software name and version number) by the deadline communicated by the tournament administration. The following config files are accepted:

- `cs2_machine_convars.vcfg`
- `cs2_user_convars_0_slot0.vcfg`
- `cs2_user_keys_0_slot0.vcfg`
- `cs2_video.txt`
- One additional `.cfg` file for required aliases
- One text file for launch options, NVIDIA/monitor settings, or a Spotify playlist

Participants who fail to submit configs must set up manually on-site without any additional time allowance. Razer tournament drivers are created on-site in the presence of tournament officials; players should bring screenshots of their home settings.

For **online events**, all participants must configure their own systems before connecting. MESA does not manage peripheral drivers for online play.

### 8.9 Forbidden In-Game Actions

The following actions are strictly prohibited and will result in sanctions up to and including disqualification:

- Moving through clipped areas, walls, floors, or ceilings not intended by map design (including sky-walking)
- Pixel-walking — standing or sitting on invisible edges or ledges
- Planting the bomb in any location where it cannot be defused by the opposing team
- Planting the bomb in any location where it does not produce the standard beeping sound
- Planting the bomb such that it is not in contact with a solid surface
- Defusing the bomb through a solid object
- Boosting to positions where textures, walls, or ceilings become transparent or penetrable
- Using any exploit that reveals information not normally visible under standard gameplay conditions
- Ghosting — receiving, sharing, or using any live match information from a spectator, GOTV feed, or any outside source during a live round
- Any form of in-game communication with persons not actively participating in the match during live rounds

### 8.10 Bugs and Glitches

The use of bugs or glitches that distort normal gameplay is strictly prohibited. The severity of punishment is determined by the nature of the exploit and its impact on the match outcome. Teams uncertain about whether a position or action is legal must consult the tournament administration **before** using it. Any positions or actions confirmed as permitted will be communicated to all teams in the pre-event briefing. Calling something a "new position" does not exempt it from these rules.

### 8.11 Incorrect Buy

Players are solely responsible for what they purchase in the buy phase. No round will be restarted or paused due to a player purchasing an item unintentionally. This applies to incorrect weapon purchases, accidental grenade purchases, and any other unintended buy.

### 8.12 Item Customisation Restrictions

Players may use CS2 weapon skins and cosmetic items freely. However:

- Item **name tags** must not contain vulgar language, sponsor or brand promotions, content that taunts opponents, or content that violates MESA's Code of Conduct.
- In-game **stickers** must not be vulgar, political, or promotional of any company, sponsor, or service not authorised by MESA.

Players in violation may be required to remove or replace the offending item before the match begins.

## 9. Conduct and Integrity

### 9.1 General Code of Conduct

All participants must behave professionally and with sportsmanship at all times. This applies in all contexts: matches, media appearances, social media, and any other environment connected to a MESA event. Participants must:

- Comply with this rulebook and any sanctions imposed by MESA.
- Participate in all allocated matches to the best of their abilities.
- Treat tournament officials, fellow participants, MESA staff, partners, press, and spectators with respect.
- Not damage or misuse MESA or venue equipment.

### 9.2 Cheating

The use of any software, hardware, or method that provides an unfair competitive advantage is strictly prohibited. This includes, but is not limited to: aim assistance software, wallhacks, lag switches, radar hacks, and any form of information abuse (receiving external callouts, using spectator feeds). MESA may employ any detection method it deems appropriate, including anti-cheat software, hardware inspection, and analysis of demos and video footage.

Punishment for cheating: disqualification, forfeiture of all prize money, and a ban of **one to two (1–2) years** from all MESA competitions. VAC bans issued within the two (2) years prior to a MESA event will be enforced.

### 9.3 Match Fixing and Competition Manipulation

Match fixing is the use of any means to influence the outcome of a match for purposes other than sporting success. Competition manipulation includes offering money or benefits, making threats, or exerting any form of pressure to influence any aspect of the competition.

Punishment: voiding of all affected results, disqualification, forfeiture of all prize money, and a ban of **five (5) years** from all MESA competitions. Aggravating circumstances may result in a lifetime ban.

### 9.4 Betting

No player, coach, team manager, or organisation representative may bet on any MESA match or share information that assists betting, either directly or indirectly. Betting against one's own organisation's matches results in immediate disqualification of the organisation and a minimum **one (1) year** ban for all persons involved.

### 9.5 Doping

The use of performance-enhancing substances is prohibited. Mild cases result in a warning and penalty points. Severe cases (e.g. use of Adderall or similar stimulants) result in nullification of results achieved under the substance's influence, a ban of **one to two (1–2) years**, and forfeiture of all prize money. If a severe case is discovered more than twenty-four (24) hours after the last match of the event, the ban applies but tournament results stand. Participants must not compete under the influence of alcohol or any psychoactive substance during active competition hours.

### 9.6 Unsportsmanlike Behaviour

Behaviours subject to sanction include: ringing or faking a player's identity, using a disallowed player, misleading tournament administration or opponents, insulting or threatening any participant or official, spamming or harassing communications, and public statements that bring MESA or any participant into disrepute.

- **Ringing/faking:** Ten (10) penalty points and a minimum two-match suspension per incident.
- **Playing a disallowed player:** Ten (10) penalty points; the opponent is offered a rematch or awarded a default win.
- **Misleading administration:** Penalty points and potential disqualification.
- **Insults:** One (1) to six (6) penalty points. Radical statements or threats of physical violence may result in immediate exclusion.

### 9.7 Publisher and ESIC Bans

MESA will enforce all active Valve Anti-Cheat (VAC) bans issued within the past two (2) years and any VAC ban issued after a player's first participation in a Valve-sponsored event. All ESIC bans are honoured and translated directly into MESA bans.

### 9.8 Limitations on Issuing Punishments

- Cheating and match fixing: punishments may be issued up to **ten (10) years** after the incident.
- Ringing, faking, and misrepresentation of identity: **five (5) years**.
- Minor infractions: may expire earlier at MESA's discretion.

## 10. Sanctions and Punishments

### 10.1 Penalty Point System

Penalty points are cumulative across an event and its qualifying stages. Each penalty point results in a **one percent (1%)** deduction from the team's total prize money for the event. Prize money is not lost to other participants; deductions are donated to a designated charity at year's end.

### 10.2 Fines

Fines are deducted directly from the team's prize money and are not redistributed to other participants.

### 10.3 Bans and Suspensions

Bans may be issued for a defined number of matches, a single event, or a specified number of years. Match bans apply to the participant's next available match on the MESA circuit. Event bans apply to the participant's next full event; if the ban is issued mid-event, it covers the remainder of the current event plus the first three matches of the next.

### 10.4 Disqualification

Disqualified teams or players forfeit all accumulated results and prize money from the event. Disqualification may result from repeated or severe violations of this rulebook.

### 10.5 Repeat Offenders

Participants who commit the same type of offence multiple times in a single competition will have their standard punishments increased. The tournament administration may also take into account prior sanctions from other competitions.

## 11. Prize Money and Financial Obligations

### 11.1 Payment Requirements

Teams must provide accurate bank transfer details and any required documentation (invoices, tax documents) to receive prize money. Failure to provide correct payment information delays payment until the situation is rectified.

### 11.2 Withholding Prize Money

MESA reserves the right to cancel or delay any prize payment if evidence of fraud, match fixing, or foul play is discovered before the payment is processed.

### 11.3 Ongoing Investigations

If an investigation into a participant is ongoing at the time prize money is due, payment will be withheld until the investigation concludes. If the investigation concludes more than three years after the competition, and the finding is that the participant should have received prize money, payment will still be executed.

### 11.4 Forfeiture

If a participant has not claimed their prize money within **one (1) year** of the initial payment date, the prize is forfeited.

### 11.5 Prize Money and Penalty Points

Penalty points and fines are deducted from prize money before payment is issued. The deduction is calculated on the total prize money awarded at the final stage of the competition.

## 12. Withdrawal, Replacement, and Forfeiture

### 12.1 Withdrawal

A participant is considered to have withdrawn if they leave during an ongoing event stage, between stages (including between qualifier and main event), or after accepting an invitation. Consequences of withdrawal include:

- Forfeiture of all accumulated prize money for the event.
- A ban from receiving a VRS-based invitation to the next event of the same tier.
- A fine to cover the cost of replacement (including travel expenses for the replacement team).
- Possible additional penalties at MESA's discretion, particularly for late-notice withdrawals.

Exceptions: MESA will waive financial penalties (excluding replacement costs) in cases of genuine medical emergency or visa complications, provided that:

- For medical emergencies: a letter from a licensed doctor explicitly stating the participant cannot travel must be submitted.
- For visa complications: official documentation confirming the visa was denied for reasons outside the participant's control must be provided.

### 12.2 Leaving During the Playoff Stage

A team that forfeits an elimination match during the playoff stage is treated as having left the event. If the forfeit occurs before the first playoff round, the next highest-ranked team from the previous stage replaces them. If the forfeit occurs in a later round, the forfeiting team receives a final placing equivalent to having lost their previous match, and earns only the prize money associated with that placing.

All matches involving a team that has left a stage after it began are reset and deleted. In bracket formats, the most recent or next upcoming match of the withdrawn team is awarded to their opponent as a default win.

### 12.3 Replacements

When a spot becomes vacant due to withdrawal or disqualification, MESA will identify and contact a replacement team. Competition groups and brackets are generally not reseeded after a public announcement has been made, unless MESA determines reseeding is necessary to preserve fairness.

**Replacement hierarchy:**

- If a team withdraws **more than ten (10) days** before the event, the tournament is reseeded.
- If a team withdraws **within ten (10) days**, the replacement team inherits the dropped team's position and is assigned the lowest available seed for map veto purposes.
- If the withdrawal is from a **VRS-invited slot more than two months** before the event, the slot goes to the next highest-ranked team on the VRS at the time of withdrawal.
- If the withdrawal is from a **VRS-invited slot within two months** of the event, only teams with confirmed valid visas for the tournament region are eligible for replacement. Invited replacements have twenty-four (24) hours to provide proof of visa.
- If the withdrawal is from a **qualifier slot**, the slot passes to the next highest-placed team from the relevant qualifier. For last-minute replacements within two weeks of the event, the slot is awarded to the highest-ranked team on the VRS from the tournament's host region with a valid visa.

### 12.4 Replacement Tiebreaker

If replacement candidates have identical positions on the invitation list, preference is given to the team with the higher VRS ranking. If a tie persists, MESA may use additional criteria or a random draw.

## 13. Appeals and Protests

### 13.1 Right to Appeal

Participants may appeal rulings that are not classified as non-appealable. Non-appealable decisions include: all decisions made by tournament officials during a live match, decisions relating to three (3) or fewer penalty points, and any sanction the participant has already accepted.

### 13.2 Appeal Process

An appeal must be submitted to the tournament administration in writing within the timeframe specified in the event documentation. The submission must clearly state the grounds for the appeal and include all supporting evidence. MESA will consider the submission in good faith and issue a written ruling. MESA reserves the right to request additional information or oral submissions.

### 13.3 Final Authority

The tournament administration's decisions on matters not covered by this rulebook are final. In all cases, MESA's rulings are guided by the principles of fair play, competitive integrity, and sportsmanship.
